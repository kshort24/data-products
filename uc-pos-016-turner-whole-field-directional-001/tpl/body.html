The answer first

Three questions, three answers

Is Turner over-pulling outside-zone pitches in 2026?
No.
39.4% pull on out-of-zone contact (n = 71) vs 43.3% pooled 2023–25 (n = 240). z = −0.58 — indistinguishable from noise, and pointing the opposite way from the premise. Underpowered, not disproven.
Is there a real directional shift anywhere?
Yes — in the zone.
In-zone pull rate 40.1% vs 47.5% (z = −2.52), oppo 35.1% vs 29.5% (z = +2.04). One zone category away from where the premise looked.
Does that shift explain the down year?
Mostly not — about a fifth.
In-zone total bases per ball in play fell .622 → .515. The directional mix change accounts for 20% of that; 86% is every direction producing less than it used to. DC‑1, new in v1.1.0.
What changed since v1.0.0, and why. The chart specified in v1 as a “spray chart” was built as a pitch map (plate_x/plate_z) — a human-in-the-loop specification error the DPO declared himself. Both views now ship, cross-linked on a shared pitch key, because the link between them is where the finding physically lives. v1’s open decision “static facet grid vs. animated frames” has been answered: season frames. The v1.0.0 finding itself is unchanged and was not re-litigated.

Why this page normalizes run creation RC‑1

The requested cell plots runs created — a counting stat — against barrel rate, a rate. Playing time then becomes the loudest signal on the y axis and the reader charges it to the x axis: a full-time .060‑barrel hitter always sits above a part-time .150‑barrel hitter, and the chart reads as if barrel rate buys runs.

RC‑1 puts both axes on the same footing. It does not replace the raw count — the toggle above keeps both, because the counting stat is the right answer to who drove in the most runs and the wrong answer to who creates runs best for his contact profile. On the normalized axis Turner’s 2026 is the lowest of his four Phillies seasons; on the raw axis it is not obviously anything.

Scene 3 — the control

Did the hitter move, or did the pitchers?

A batted-ball direction change means nothing on its own: if opponents changed where they threw, the ball would go somewhere else without the hitter changing at all. So the attack plan is held up as a control. PM‑1 compares Turner’s 2026 pitch-location distribution against pooled 2023–25 — once for the whole plate, then conditionally inside the zone and outside it, because a pooled test can be significant off an out-of-zone-only change and be read as if it licensed an in-zone explanation.

In-zone shift, decomposed by where the pitch was

If the pull loss were driven by one part of the zone, it would concentrate. It does not: pull rate is down between 5.8 and 8.7 points in every row and every column of the strike zone, and no single cell clears the band on its own. The aggregate clears it because the sub-effects all point the same way — the signature of a whole-zone change, not a location-specific one.

Honest limits on this build
  • The out-of-zone test is underpowered: 71 balls in play in 2026 against 240 pooled. A true effect the size the premise supposed could exist here without clearing the band. Absence of evidence, not evidence of absence.
  • Turner is stand == 'R' on 100% of these rows. The left-handed branch of hit_direction and sort_rank is implemented and documented but never exercised, so it is untested by this build.
  • PM‑1 is a distributional test on pitch location only. It does not control for pitch mix, sequencing, velocity, or who was on the mound. “The in-zone attack plan held” means locations held, nothing more.
  • DC‑1 compares one season against a three-season pool and is a decomposition of point estimates. It carries no uncertainty band and it is not a causal claim.
  • Season-over-season comparisons pool across parks and opponents. No park or opponent-quality control is applied; the loanDepot venue study (uc-pos-007) is the precedent for how much that can matter.
  • This remains a diagnostic-tier build with a spot-verification harness, not the exhaustive independent-recompute harness a full certification-tier hitter season gets. That gap is priced, not hidden.
