Three constraints that shape every claim below

Pitch-call attribution is not in the data. Statcast carries no PitchCom sender field. This measures what was thrown, never who chose it. Where the report reasons about who is driving a change, it does so from a design — does the change appear without Stubbs? — not from a field that does not exist.

Catcher assignment is not random. Stubbs is the backup; his starts differ in opponent, venue, rest and date. A confound panel and an opponent-difficulty control ship with every split.

The samples are small and they are printed. Stubbs has caught Nola 31 times in four seasons, 8 of them in 2026. Every rate below rides with its PA or pitch count.

01

The bottom line

Six findings, in the order a pitching coach would want them.

01

The game plan changed — and it is Nola's change, not Stubbs's

Since July 5: changeup 12.7% → 22.8%, two-strike fastball 47.8% → 35.4%, changeup when behind 12.5% → 28.2%. Ten of twelve approach metrics move the same direction with and without Stubbs behind the plate.

02

What Stubbs adds is degree, not direction

He carries the most extreme version of the same plan — changeup 25.0% in his starts since 7/5 against 19.8% for the others. Two things don't travel: the knuckle-curve retreat and a 4.4-point chase gain. Both are thin.

03

The results follow, and the slate objection fails

2026 wOBA allowed: .310 with Stubbs (8 GS), .363 Realmuto, .391 Marchán. The rest of the staff allowed .319 against Stubbs's opponents and .300 against Realmuto's. His slate was the harder one.

04

But the career panel says: careful

Over four seasons the battery is a coin flip — Stubbs .307, Realmuto .300. In 2024 they split Nola's starts 16–16 and finished .315 / .304. The 2026 gap is new, and 8 starts cannot tell you why.

05

July's prescription was adopted — three tripwires of four

Changeup to lefties .179 → .274, lefty walk rate .119 → .068, lefty wOBA .385 → .313. The fourth did not move: first-pitch strikes to lefties are still 58%. He escapes those counts now; he doesn't avoid them.

06

The bill is coming due on the other side

Against righties the changeup is new (4.5% → 12.7%) and it is costing him: walk rate 1.9% → 7.4%, wOBA .340 → .369. Home runs per PA are 5.6% since 7/5. The strikeouts are real; the damage is not solved.

02

The travel test

The mechanism question, answered by design rather than by correlation.

Nola has PitchCom on his belt and can call his own pitches. Stubbs has been back there for most of a good stretch. Which of them is the game plan?

There is no pitch-call field to read, and catcher assignment isn't random — so a Stubbs-versus-others comparison confounds the catcher with everything else that differs about those games. The design that works instead: hold the time split fixed and ask whether each approach change shows up inside both strata. A change that appears in the non-Stubbs starts cannot have been caused by Stubbs being there — he wasn't.

The two that don't travel, examined honestly

The knuckle-curve retreat looks like the strongest battery signature in the file — until you look start by start. Stubbs's four starts since 7/5 run 20.4% (7/28), 19.6% (8/3), 28.9% (8/8), 34.0% (8/19). The last of those is above his career rate. Two starts is a plan for two lineups, not a philosophy.

The chase-rate gain (+4.4 against +0.3) is the more interesting survivor, and it is what a weakness-exploitation catcher should produce: more pitches designed to be chased, more chases. It is also 485 pitches. Call it a hypothesis and test it forward.

Is the breakpoint doing the work?

An era boundary is a researcher's degree of freedom, so it gets scanned rather than asserted. Across all eight candidate boundaries from 6/18 to 7/28 the changeup delta lands between +9.0 and +10.2 points and never changes sign. No headline here depends on where the line is drawn.

03

The changeup ramp

The largest composition change of Nola's 2026 — and it does not begin where the narrative expects.

Career context matters: this is a return, not an invention. Nola's changeup share by season runs .115 (2015) → .198 (2018) → .274 (2020) → .096 (2024) → .162 (2026). The second-half rate sits between his 2018 and 2020 selves.

And the pitch itself did not change — the usage did. Same velocity (85.7 → 85.5), same zone rate (33.9% → 34.6%), and the whiff rate against lefties jumped from 20.8% to 32.0%. That is a sequencing and expectation effect. But when it is hit, it's hit harder than before — xwOBAcon .285 → .351 on 26 balls in play. Whiff or damage, with less in between.

The walk leak closed without the first-pitch problem being solved. He still falls behind lefties at the same rate — he now escapes those counts instead of walking out of them, on a changeup-when-behind rate that went 16.0% → 31.4% against left-handed hitters. That is a real skill and a real result. It is also a fix applied downstream of the failure, which means it depends on the changeup continuing to miss bats.

06

Tonight

A Mariners advance was priced and not taken — this is a battery product, not a lineup product. What the frame does hold, and the three things to watch.

Nola has faced Seattle twice, both on the road, both a lifetime ago: 2017-06-27 (7.0 IP, 28 PA, .308, Rupp catching) and 2022-05-10 (5.1 IP, 27 PA, .334, Realmuto). Neither roster survives. The Phillies have seen Seattle once in 2026 — the series opener on 8/24, 39 PA, in which Seattle put up a .483 wOBA against the staff on a 21 L / 18 R split. One game. It is context, not a read.

  1. First pitch to lefties

    The one tripwire that hasn't moved (58.1%). If he gets ahead of left-handed hitters tonight, the changeup escape becomes a luxury instead of a necessity.

  2. The changeup to righties

    12.7% usage, 7.4% walk rate — up from a near-perfect 1.9%. If the walks to righties show up again, the plan is over-extended.

  3. The knuckle curve's share with Stubbs

    20% in the first two starts of the pairing, back to 29% and 34% in the last two. If it stays at 34%, the "Stubbs shelves the curve" hypothesis is dead and should be retired rather than carried.

07

What this does not tell you

Stated up front rather than discovered later.

  • L-1Who called any pitch. No PitchCom sender field exists. The travel test bounds the question; it does not resolve it.
  • L-2Whether Stubbs causes the 2026 gap. Catcher assignment is non-random and 8 starts is not a design. The opponent control rules out one confound, not all of them.
  • L-3Catcher framing, blocking or throwing. No catcher-defence model in this data plane.
  • L-4Anything about tonight's Seattle lineup. Explicitly de-scoped.
  • L-5Sequence effects beyond adjacent pairs. Repeat-pitch rate sees consecutive repeats; arsenal entropy is first-order. Neither sees three-pitch patterns.
  • L-6Postseason. Regular season only. Never blended.
